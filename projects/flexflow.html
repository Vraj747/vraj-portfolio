UX Design · Onboarding

FlexFlow

Onboarding for a health & fitness app, where illustration and animation had to earn their keep — not just look good.

Role

User research, UI design, illustrative design

Timeline

1 week (2 days research, 5 days design & testing)

Tools

  • Miro
  • Figma
  • Adobe XD

Overview

I designed the onboarding flow for a health and fitness app, using motion and illustration to introduce the app's features and motivate users to keep going, rather than just listing what the app does.

What usability testing changed

The first pass leaned hard into aesthetics — clustered visual elements that looked good but confused users during onboarding specifically. User journey mapping surfaced that confusion directly.

  • Reduced visual clutter without dropping the app's aesthetic identity
  • Prioritized a clearer, more navigable structure over decoration
  • Validated the revised flow against the same usability criteria that flagged the original problem

UI direction

Aesthetic appeal stayed a priority, but never at the cost of clarity — onboarding screens were built to communicate core features effectively first, with illustration supporting that goal rather than competing with it.

Learnings

This was where I learned that user journey mapping isn't just documentation — it's a diagnostic tool. It was the thing that actually revealed where the design was failing, and it's now a step I default to whenever a flow feels "off" but I can't immediately say why.
