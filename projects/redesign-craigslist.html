Redesign: Craigslist

Modernizing one of the internet's most recognizable — and most dated — marketplaces, without losing what made it work.

Role

UX design, UX research, UI design for onboarding

Timeline

10 days (4 days research, 6 days design & testing)

Tools

  • Miro
  • Figma
  • Zeplin
  • Notion

Problem

Craigslist's minimalism, once a strength, had become a liability: a cluttered, complex interface made navigation genuinely difficult, and the UI as a whole needed refurbishing without losing the platform's core simplicity.

Solution

Modernize the platform's navigation and visual design while keeping its core functionality and simplicity intact — responding directly to what users said they wanted, not redesigning for its own sake.

Reverse usability testing

Instead of testing a new prototype, I navigated users through the original Craigslist site to observe where they struggled firsthand. The biggest issue: information overload — too much clustered data on a single page, making it hard to find anything quickly.

  • Categorized layout: group similar listings together for a clearer visual structure
  • Concise information: avoid overwhelming users with excessive detail up front
  • User-friendly filters: let users narrow results instead of scanning everything
  • Whitespace: reduce cognitive load with a cleaner layout
  • Progressive disclosure: reveal information as it's needed, not all at once

UI direction

A balanced, corporate-but-modern visual style rather than a strict framework like Material Design or iOS — tailored specifically to Craigslist's existing user base, designed primarily for web across desktop, laptop, and tablet.

Learnings

This project was a lesson in restraint — the goal was never to make Craigslist look like every other modern marketplace, but to fix what was actually broken (navigation, information density) while protecting what already worked. Reverse usability testing on a live product, rather than a prototype, also became a technique I've kept using since.
