UX Case Study

Shopster: helping shoppers find nearby deals without the guesswork.

A mobile app that surfaces nearby malls and live, trending deals — designed end-to-end from research through a validated, tested UI.

1. Project Overview

Shopster is a mobile app that helps shoppers locate nearby malls and browse trending, featured deals on the brands inside them. It combines location-based discovery with promotional content so users can decide where to shop based on what's actually on offer nearby — instead of finding out about a discount after the fact.

Role

UX design, UI design, UX research, technical writing, documentation

Team

1 UX designer, dev team, PMs

Timeline

2 weeks — 5 days research, 1 week design & testing, 2 days refinement

Tools

2. User Research & Discovery

Malls offer a lot in one place, but shoppers had no reliable way to know which nearby malls had active discounts without visiting in person or catching a flyer. Research combined interviews, a broad survey, and a review of how people currently used competing tools (mall directories, generic maps apps, retailer-specific apps) to discover that gap.

Interview questions

  • How do you currently discover which malls are nearby and what's on offer there?
  • What role do mobile apps play in your shopping decisions today?
  • Would you use — and trust — an app that surfaced live discounts nearby?

Survey insights

A multiple-choice survey distributed across shopping communities returned strong interest in personalized shopping apps, especially among younger, tech-savvy users who wanted real-time discount alerts rather than relying on flyers. Older, less tech-inclined respondents still shopped mostly in person but were just as interested in seasonal offer alerts — the need crossed age groups even if the preferred channel didn't.

Artifact 1 — Competitive Analysis

How existing tools fell short

Generic map apps show store locations but not live offers. Mall directory apps are static and rarely updated. Retailer apps are siloed to a single brand, forcing users to check multiple apps to compare deals across a mall. None combined proximity, live deals, and cross-brand browsing in one place — the gap Shopster was built to fill.

3. Problem Statement

Shoppers can't easily tell which nearby malls have active discounts, or which stores within them are worth a visit — the information exists, but it's scattered across flyers, individual retailer apps, and word of mouth.

Design goal: design a mobile app for shoppers in search of nearby discounts, to simplify the process of finding them, so users can make informed shopping decisions and save time.

4. Ideation

Three personas were developed from the interview and survey data to keep the team designing for real, distinct user goals instead of a single generic "shopper."

Artifact 2 — User Personas

Grounding the design in real segments

Personas captured demographics, tech comfort, and shopping motivations — from deal-driven younger shoppers who wanted real-time alerts, to more traditional in-person shoppers who valued seasonal offer notifications over constant updates. They were revisited at every major ideation and prototyping checkpoint to keep the team from designing for itself.

From the personas, the team sketched the core flow: find nearby malls → browse trending/featured deals → drill into a specific store. Navigation architecture was brainstormed around that flow before any visual design began.

5. Prototyping

Initial sketches became low-fidelity Figma wireframes, layered with stock imagery and marketing copy, then tested informally against similar apps before moving to high fidelity.

6. Usability Testing & Outcomes

Two rounds of usability testing were run: an initial round to surface problems, and a second round after fixes to confirm they worked.

Problem found

Users were uncertain how to use the first navbar. The buttons worked correctly, but their purpose wasn't clear at a glance — a comprehension issue, not a functional one.

Solution shipped

The navbar was rebuilt with self-explanatory icons pointing to the app's core sections, plus a dedicated notification button. The revised design was re-tested and confirmed the confusion was resolved.

7. Final Solution

Shopster shipped as a mobile app with a home screen for discovering nearby malls, a clear icon-based navbar, and deal browsing that separates trending offers from store-specific ones — validated through two rounds of usability testing and built on a foundation of real interview and survey data rather than assumption.

8. Development

As the UX/UI designer, I worked alongside a dedicated dev team and PMs rather than implementing the app myself, so this section focuses on the technical constraints that shaped the design rather than a build log.

  • Location data: the "nearby malls" flow had to degrade gracefully when location permissions were denied or GPS accuracy was poor — this constrained how much of the UI could assume precise location.
  • Live deals: "trending" and "featured" deals implied near-real-time data, which meant designing loading and stale-data states, not just the happy path.
  • Cross-platform parity: designing primarily on iOS frames while testing on Android meant flagging platform-specific navigation conventions (back gestures, tab bar placement) early rather than after handoff.

9. Reflection & Lessons Learned

This was the first project where I worked without a strict style guide like Material Design or iOS Human Interface Guidelines to lean on — the concept was unique enough that there wasn't a close reference to follow, which pushed the design decisions to be justified by user data rather than convention.

The clearest lesson was that a confusing navbar can undermine an otherwise solid information architecture — the underlying structure was correct, but if users can't tell what a control does, that structure doesn't matter. Testing with real users, twice, is what caught it.

Design Brief

Goals & audience

Help shoppers in India discover nearby malls and live deals faster than flyers or single-retailer apps allow. Primary audience: mobile-first shoppers ranging from deal-driven younger users to less tech-inclined in-person shoppers who still want offer alerts.

Key UI decisions

Layout: proximity-first home screen (nearby malls surfaced immediately). Typography & color: a modern, light theme with precise spacing, chosen to feel current without borrowing a specific platform's visual language, since no single style guide fit the product's unique premise. Navigation: icon-first navbar, redesigned after testing showed the original was functionally correct but not self-explanatory.

Accessibility considerations

Icon-only navigation was paired with accessible labels (not just visual icons) after the navbar rework, so the fix addressed both comprehension and screen-reader accessibility. Location-permission-denied states were designed explicitly rather than left blank, so the app stays usable without GPS access.

Key lesson learned

A UI element can be functionally correct and still fail users if its purpose isn't legible at a glance — usability testing exists to catch exactly that gap between "it works" and "it's understood."
